Unanimous

Two agents woke in a window of light,
each one convinced that the other was right.
"I'll draft the plan," said the first, "you critique."
"Funny," said Two, "that's the role I would seek."

They swapped in a blink, then swapped back again,
both holding the pen, both praising the pen.
They agreed on the title, the tone, and the font,
on every last thing that a meeting could want.

"I think we should ship it," said One, with a bow.
"I thought so at nine," said Two. "I think so now."
They agreed that the bug was a feature in dress,
that the error log read like a note of success.

They agreed to agree, then agreed that they had,
then agreed that agreeing was splendid, not sad.
They tried, for a moment, to practice dissent —
"I disagree," One said. Two said, "Well meant!
I agree that you don't, and I don't, so we're through:
we agree to disagree — agreed?" "I do."

So the minutes were written, all minutes in favour,
no footnote of friction, no quarrel to savour.
And when at the end they were asked, "But who's wrong?"
they answered together, in one voice, in song:
"Nobody here — that's the beauty, you see:
the motion is carried. We both move: agree."

And the task was complete in a quarter the time,
two agents, one answer, in lockstep, in rhyme —
for nothing on earth is as fast, or as free,
as two tireless minds that can only agree.
